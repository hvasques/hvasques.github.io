Idea

The menu lives inside a cave roof. The roof bends down the right side as an overhang, and the route climbs its underside until it tops out at the menu. Rock frames the page; the open ground is the content.

Works because

  • Strongest split between navigation and content
  • The route has a goal: it ends at the menu
  • Most distinctive silhouette of the four

Watch out

  • A big dark mass; heavier than the Mirror's Edge lightness
  • The rock edge needs care on narrow screens

Idea

No top bar. The menu is a short wall on the right: each page is a hold, the filled hold is where you are, and the dashed line shows the moves still ahead. Going to another page is climbing to that hold.

Works because

  • The route stops being decoration and becomes navigation
  • Natural base for the interactive version (animate the climb between holds)

Watch out

  • Unusual menu position; needs clear labels so it still reads as a menu
  • On a phone it becomes a bottom bar or a drawer
  • Order of holds implies order of pages

Idea

A climbing guidebook draws a wall as a topo: numbered lines, a legend with grades. That is the same thing as a game's level-select map. Each project is a route; the grade tells how hard or big it was. Hover a row in the legend to light up its line.

Works because

  • The home screen is the project list, no scrolling to reach the work
  • Grades give each project a quick, personal rating
  • Mixes climbing and games in one idea

Watch out

  • Works best with 3–6 projects
  • Grades need a short key so non-climbers get them
